# Machine Learning prediction of Protein Nuclear Localisation
This project was inspired by the challenges of delivering drug molecules to intracellular targets, particularly nuclear proteins. Predicting protein nuclear localisation could aid the investigation of subcellular distribution of potential drug targets.
## Data
All models were trained and cross-validated using homology-partitioned subsets of the DeepLoc 2.0 training dataset, derived from experimentally annotated UniProt proteins. The independent test set was also the test set released with DeepLoc 2.0, a subset of Human Protein Atlas proteins (Thumuluri, et al., 2022).
## Models
At the initial stages, I experimented with different methods of feature engineering to convert an amino acid sequence to numerical input data. As expected, the classification models generally performed better when more positional information was preserved (e.g. one-hot encoding) compared to amino acid composition alone. While further analysis of the fitted parameters is required to identify which sequence features were associated with nuclear localisation, amino acid composition likely failed to transfer information from local sequence patterns. For example, histones contain positively charged residues, particularly lysine and arginine, that contribute to interactions with the negatively charged DNA backbone. The distribution of these residues in specific regions may be biologically relevant, but this information is lost when expressed as overall amino acid compositions. Furthermore, variation in amino acid composition across different regions, for proteins with different evolutionary origins, structures and functions, could introduce a high amount of noise into composition-based representations, further reducing the transmission of information about shorter, local patterns.

The 3 models trained were logistic regression, a fully connected neural network and a convolutional neural network (CNN), as well as an exercise implementing logistic regression from scratch in Python and comparing its results to the logistic regression model built using scikit-learn. The CNN achieved the highest test-set accuracy (70%) and F1 score (0.71). Further analysis is required to identify which short sequences contribute to predictions, and particularly whether they correspond to any known nuclear localisation sequences.
